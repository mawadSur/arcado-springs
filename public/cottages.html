The idea

Right-sized homes for the neighbors we already have.

Many longtime residents around Arcado Road love the neighborhood but no longer need the house — the stairs, the yard, the upkeep. This study looks at a quiet cottage court of roughly 44–50 detached, single-story homes reserved for ages 55 and better: a way to stay close to the church, the doctor, the grandkids, and the streets you know, in a home that finally fits.

Every home would be one level with a zero-step entry, on a small low-maintenance homesite, arranged along a garden lane that ends at the preserved creek woods. It is being studied alongside the town-center concept shown on the main overview, and would require its own survey work, engineering, and a rezoning before anything could move forward.

Architecture

Small homes, deep porches, honest materials.

The architectural character under study is Southern cottage: board-and-batten and lap siding in warm whites, sages, and soft clays; gabled rooflines; stone piers and cedar posts; and a real front porch on every home — close enough to the lane to say good morning.

Interiors

One big, bright room at the heart of it.

The whole idea of the cottage is that daily life happens in one generous, light-filled space — kitchen, table, and sitting room together under a vaulted ceiling, with wide doorways and no thresholds to think about.

The site concept

A garden lane that ends at the creek woods.

Arcado Rd SW, Lilburn, GA — View on Google Maps

  • One quiet entrance

    A single landscaped entry from Arcado Road, studied with a dedicated left-turn lane so neighborhood traffic keeps moving.

  • The woods stay

    The creek corridor at the back of the site would remain undisturbed wooded buffer — the springs behind the name, kept as they are.

  • A respectful edge

    A planted landscape buffer along every neighboring backyard, with one-story rooflines that sit lower than the trees around them.

  • The green up front

    Clubhouse, village green, and pickleball courts near the entrance — the social heart of the lane, and its front door.

Why this option is on the table

The gentlest thing that could be built here.

  • No added school enrollment

    Homes reserved for ages 55 and better add zero students to nearby schools — the single biggest concern neighbors raised about earlier concepts for this land.

  • Lighter on Arcado Road

    Age-55+ households drive markedly less — on the order of 40% fewer daily trips than conventional single-family homes — and mostly outside rush hour.

  • Neighbors staying neighbors

    Longtime residents who want one level and less yard shouldn’t have to leave the area to find it. Cottages let people right-size without moving away.

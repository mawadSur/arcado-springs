The idea

Right-sized homes for the neighbors we already have.

Many longtime residents around Arcado Road love the neighborhood but no longer need the house — the stairs, the yard, the upkeep. This study looks at a quiet cottage court of 41 detached, single-story homes reserved for ages 55 and better: a way to stay close to the church, the doctor, the grandkids, and the streets you know, in a home that finally fits.

Every home would be one level with a zero-step entry, on a small low-maintenance homesite, arranged along a single garden lane that ends in a green rather than a cul-de-sac, with the creek woods kept as they are. It is being studied alongside the town-center concept shown on the main overview, and would require its own survey work, engineering, and a rezoning before anything could move forward.

Architecture

Small homes, deep porches, honest materials.

The architectural character under study is Southern cottage: board-and-batten and lap siding in warm whites, sages, and soft clays; gabled rooflines; stone piers and cedar posts; and a real front porch on every home — close enough to the lane to say good morning.

Living

One big room — and the door straight out of it.

The whole idea of the cottage is that daily life happens in one generous, light-filled space — kitchen, table, and sitting room together under a vaulted ceiling, with wide doorways and no thresholds to think about. On Plan B that room opens through french doors onto a covered porch tucked back under the roof, facing the trees rather than the lane.

The site concept

One garden lane, and the creek woods left alone.

Arcado Rd SW, Lilburn, GA — View on Google Maps

  • One quiet entrance

    One landscaped entry from Arcado Road, set about 250 feet from Arcadia Place — the spacing the county’s access rules ask for. A traffic study will be filed with the rezoning and will decide what, if anything, the entrance needs.

  • The woods stay

    The creek head and the woods around it, along the southwest side toward the far end, would stay in an undisturbed common tract — the springs behind the name, kept as they are.

  • A respectful edge

    A 20-foot undisturbed buffer of the existing trees along every neighboring property line — planted up where the woods are thin — with one-story rooflines that sit lower than the trees around them.

  • The green up front

    Clubhouse, village green, and pickleball courts near the entrance — the social heart of the lane, and its front door.

Why this option is on the table

The gentlest thing that could be built here.

  • No added school enrollment

    Homes reserved for ages 55 and better add zero students to nearby schools — the single biggest concern neighbors raised about earlier concepts for this land.

  • Lighter on Arcado Road

    Age-55+ households drive markedly less, and mostly outside rush hour. Against the conventional subdivision this land already allows, the study estimates roughly half the daily trips and about a third of the evening peak — an estimate only, until the traffic study filed with the rezoning is done on fresh counts.

  • Neighbors staying neighbors

    Longtime residents who want one level and less yard shouldn’t have to leave the area to find it. Cottages let people right-size without moving away.
